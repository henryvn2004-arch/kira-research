Vietnam's car-battery replacement channel in 2026: who picks the brand, and what makes a dealer push a new one

Modelled interview. Built from KIRA's in-depth interviews with more than 50 battery distributors, dealers and garages in Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City, set in today's market. How the channel works comes from what that trade told our analysts; every figure comes from a 2024–2026 source, marked next to the answer.

Summary

  • The replacement pool keeps growing: more than seven million cars are on the road, and 2025 set a record for new sales. 1 2
  • The shop still picks the brand for most drivers, and factory fit repeats at replacement. With VinFast at 29% of new sales and Pinaco on 100% of its lead-acid batteries, that loop favours the local leader. 3 4 2 5
  • Two channel models persist: the local leader fixes retail and pays on target; the Japanese brand and imports let the shop set the price. 6 7
  • Zero ASEAN tariffs let imports land cheap, but importer-led marketing still leaves them weak in the shop. 8 9
  • Since 2024 every importer carries a battery recycling obligation of 12% or a fund payment, a new fixed cost for small brands. 10
  • A newcomer gets pushed for margin room at the same shelf price, stock and warranty support, and fleets first. 11 12 13

Transcript

01Business context

KAKIRA Analyst

Let's start with demand. How has the car-battery business moved over the last couple of years?

EXExpert

The base keeps getting bigger. There are more than seven million cars on the road now, 7,134,939 after the first half of this year. 1 Last year was a record for new cars, 604,054 sold, and every one of those comes back for a battery in a few years. 2

KAKIRA Analyst

And for a house like yours, how big a part of the business is it?

EXExpert

It's one line among several. We sell tyres and motorcycle batteries to the same garages and sub-dealers. 14 Margins are not easy. Lead prices move and competition is tight, even the biggest local maker said so in its own guidance. 15

02Who picks the brand

KAKIRA Analyst

When a driver needs a new battery, who really chooses the brand?

EXExpert

Mostly the shop. The driver asks "which one is good?" and takes what we say. 3 The other group asks for whatever was in the car from the factory. 4

KAKIRA Analyst

VinFast is now the biggest seller. Does that change the replacement picture?

EXExpert

A lot. VinFast took 29% of new sales last year, 175,099 cars. 2 Pinaco supplies 100% of the lead-acid batteries in those cars. 5 So when they come back for a replacement, the customer asks for the same brand, which puts the local leader in a very good position. 4

03Purchase criteria and price

KAKIRA Analyst

Customers say quality matters most. Is that what you see at the counter?

EXExpert

Nobody can see quality in a battery. They all look the same, so it comes down to brand and how easy the warranty is. 16 With fleets it is even clearer. They care about how fast a failed battery gets replaced, more than a small price difference. 13

KAKIRA Analyst

Which price does everyone compare against?

EXExpert

The local leader's price. Everyone prices around it. 17 And it moves. When lead goes up or down, the price lists change several times a year. 18

04Channel economics

KAKIRA Analyst

How do the brands you carry work with you on price and margin?

EXExpert

I won't give you our margins. But there are two ways. The local leader fixes the retail price and pays you a margin that depends on hitting target. 6 The Japanese brand and most imports sell to you at a negotiated price and let you set the shelf price yourself. 7

KAKIRA Analyst

How big is the local leader's reach?

EXExpert

Very big. People in the trade quote around 200 distributors and more than 17,000 outlets. 19 Most of its output goes into exactly this replacement channel, about 70%, with 20% exported and only 10% to car makers. 20

05Imports and the tariff change

KAKIRA Analyst

ASEAN tariffs are gone. Has that brought in more imported brands?

EXExpert

Yes, there's no duty on ASEAN-made vehicle parts since 2018, so a battery from a plant in the region lands cheap. 8 But price is only half of it. Most imported brands still leave the marketing to the importer, and nobody builds a relationship with shops like mine. 9

KAKIRA Analyst

What about the Korean brands?

EXExpert

They mostly play in maintenance-free batteries for passenger cars, through retail. 21 Good product, but the same problem: the brand isn't in the shop, only the importer is. 9

06Regulation: the recycling obligation

KAKIRA Analyst

Has regulation changed how the business works?

EXExpert

The recycling rule is the big one. Since January 2024 anyone who makes or imports batteries has to recycle them, 12% for the first 3 years, or pay into the Environment Protection Fund instead. 10 For a big maker that is a line in the budget. For a small importer it is another cost on every kilo they bring in. 10

07Technology and new entrants

KAKIRA Analyst

Where does lithium and the EV wave leave a lead-acid distributor?

EXExpert

Even the electric cars still carry a twelve-volt lead-acid battery, and the local leader supplies them. 5 It is also planning lithium production and a 1 trillion VND expansion at An Phuoc. 5 Then there's BYD with Kim Long Motor: a US$130 million EV battery plant, 3 GWh in the first phase, aimed at buses and trucks, which is a different product from what I sell. 22

08Advice to a new entrant

KAKIRA Analyst

If a Japanese or Korean brand came to you tomorrow, what would make you push it?

EXExpert

First, room. Keep the shelf price in line with the others, but give the distributor a better price, so we earn more on each unit. 11 Second, support: help us check stock, recharge batteries that sit too long, and settle warranty claims quickly. 12 Third, go to the fleets early. They buy on service. 13

KAKIRA Analyst

Last question. Is it an easy market to break into?

EXExpert

No. The local leader lives on this channel: 70% of its output is replacement here. 20 The Japanese brand has been making batteries here since 1997, in Binh Duong. 23 A newcomer has to win the shop, not the driver. 3
