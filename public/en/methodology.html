METHODOLOGY

How every report is researched, written and reviewed.

Our reports are desk research. We work from public and licensed sources, tag the source of every figure, and review each report before it is published. This page explains each step and its limits.

— Desk research
We work from published and licensed sources. We do not run fieldwork, surveys or interviews.
— Transparency
We show where each number comes from, where we estimate, and where the evidence is thin.
— Review
Every report is reviewed by our research team before it is published.
01

Start from the investor's question.

Each report is planned around a decision a foreign investor faces in one country and one industry. The stage sets the question: explore (is this market worth a closer look?), enter (how do we get in?) or expand (how do we grow after entry?).

Topics are proposed from our coverage plan and approved by our team before research starts. The plan lists the questions the report must answer, and the report is built to answer them.

What the plan fixes up front

  • The investor's stage and the decision behind the report
  • The guiding questions each section must answer
  • The analyses used to answer them (sizing, competition, entry routes, regulation, costs)
  • The scope: country, industry and, where useful, a narrower segment
02

Desk research from public and licensed sources.

We gather current data on market size, demand, competitors, regulation and recent deals before each report is written. Sources include government statistics, regulators, company filings and announcements, industry associations, trade and local-language media, and licensed data.

Where sources disagree, we say so. Where we estimate, we say so and show how.

Sourcing standards

  • A source tag on every reported figure
  • Local-language sources used for in-country data where available
  • Material figures compared across sources where more than one exists
  • An explicit "est." label on numbers we model ourselves
03

AI-assisted drafting.

We use AI tools in our workflow: to search and summarise source material, structure data tables, draft sections against the research plan, and translate between English, Japanese, Korean and Chinese.

AI speeds up the work. It does not decide what a number is. Figures must trace back to a tagged source, and anything that cannot be sourced is removed or labelled as an estimate.

Where AI is used

  • Searching and summarising published sources
  • Drafting sections under the research plan
  • Building tables and charts from sourced data
  • Translating the English edition into Japanese, Korean and Chinese
04

Review before publication.

Every report is reviewed by our research team before it goes out. The review checks that figures match their sources, that conclusions follow from the evidence, and that the report answers the questions it set out to answer.

Japanese, Korean and Chinese editions are translated from the reviewed English edition. Figures, names and sources stay the same across languages.

Review checklist

  • Reported figures checked against their tagged source
  • Unsourced claims removed or marked as estimates
  • Regulatory and market claims checked for currency
  • Guiding questions answered, conclusions supported
  • Final read for clarity and tone

What we're transparent about.

Desk research is good at some things and weak at others. Here is the honest split, so you know when to rely on a report and when to check further.

Where we're strong

  • Structured answers to entry questions: size, demand, competitors, rules, routes in
  • Competitive landscapes built from filings, announcements and published data
  • Traceable figures, each with a source you can check
  • Consistent structure across countries, so markets can be compared

Where we acknowledge limits

  • No primary fieldwork: we do not run interviews, surveys or channel checks, so on-the-ground detail depends on what has been published
  • Forecasts are estimates: directional, not precise to a percentage point
  • Private company financials are often not public, and we mark estimates clearly
  • Events after the last-refresh date are not covered until the report is refreshed

Quality checks on every report.

QA · 01

Source tagging

Every reported number carries a source tag. Untraceable figures do not go out as facts.

QA · 02

Team review

Our research team reads the report before publication. Weak claims are sharpened or removed.

QA · 03

Consistent translations

Language editions come from the reviewed English edition, with the same figures and sources.

QA · 04

Refresh dates

Each report shows its last-refresh date, so you know how current it is.
