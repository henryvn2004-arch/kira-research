How we built this view

This view is built from two evidence streams: primary inputs — licensing-desk reads, custody operator conversations, and analyst synthesis — and secondary anchors — the regulator, exchange and issuer disclosures, and law-firm regulatory commentary. Every quantitative claim carries an explicit source tag.

Primary inputs

→ Licensing-desk reads
Conversations with compliance and licensing advisers on application throughput, rejection patterns, and the observed cost of maintaining a live permission.
→ Custody operator reads
Reads from bank-owned and independent custody operators on mandate pipeline, fee structure, and the segregation architecture institutional allocators now require.
→ Analyst synthesis
KIRA in-house triangulation across multiple disclosed inputs where no single source covers the figure. Tagged inline as [Kira estimates].

Secondary anchors

→ Regulator & statute
Monetary Authority of Singapore media releases, guidelines and consultation responses; the Payment Services Act and Financial Services and Markets Act instruments — cited inline by named source.
→ Operator disclosures
Exchange, bank and fund-manager announcements on volumes, licences, tokenised products and custody launches — cited by source and period.
→ Triangulated estimates
Where a figure is unpublished — custody assets, fee levels, compliance run-rate — we combine inputs and disclose the method. Tagged [Kira estimates].

A perimeter drawn deliberately tight

Part 9 of the Financial Services and Markets Act took effect on 30 June 2025, capturing digital token service providers booked in Singapore but serving only offshore customers [MAS DTSP 2025]. The regulator stated it would license such models only in extremely limited circumstances and granted no transitional relief [MAS DTSP 2025]. Thirty-seven firms held an active major payment institution permission for digital payment token services in June 2026 [MPI Register 2026]; we put rejected or withdrawn applications across the 2024-2025 cycle above 100 [Kira estimates].

Supervision with a price tag

Three licensed providers were fined S$4.8 m in March 2026 for anti-money-laundering control deficiencies [MAS Enf 2026], following S$27.45 m across nine institutions in July 2025 [MAS Enf 2025]. Compliance is now a run-rate line item — we estimate S$2.4-3.8 m annually for a mid-sized licensee [Kira estimates] — not a tail risk to be provisioned once.

Section 05 · Licence Architecture
007 / 018

Four regimes, one perimeter: where an activity actually lands

The binding question in Singapore is not whether a token is regulated but which statute catches the activity. Payments, capital markets, offshore-facing service provision and stablecoin issuance each sit under a different instrument.

Payments is the default gate

Dealing in, exchanging and now safekeeping digital payment tokens is a payment service. Providers hold a major payment institution permission and carry segregation, cold-storage and audit duties [MAS PSA Custody 2023]. Thirty-seven firms held that permission in June 2026 [MPI Register 2026].

Capital-markets tokens sit elsewhere

Tokens representing capital-markets products fall under securities law rather than the payments regime — the reason tokenised funds and bonds are authorised as schemes rather than licensed as token services [Franklin Templeton 2025].

The offshore gap closed in 2025

Part 9 of the Financial Services and Markets Act, in force 30 June 2025, extended the perimeter to firms serving only non-Singapore customers [MAS DTSP 2025]. Statutory penalties reach a S$250,000 fine and imprisonment [FSMA DTSP Regs 2025].

Stablecoin issuance is next

A dedicated issuance statute is in drafting for 2026 [MAS wCBDC 2025], layering on top of the 2023 framework rather than replacing the payments gate.

Section 05 · Licensing Outcomes
008 / 018

The shakeout: a smaller, heavier, onshore-anchored population

The 2025 perimeter extension removed the light-touch booking model. What remains skews toward bank-affiliated and institution-facing operators with real onshore substance — and toward Dubai and Hong Kong for everyone else.

No transition, by design

The regulator declined any transitional arrangement: firms serving only offshore customers had to cease or be licensed on 30 June 2025 [MAS DTSP 2025]. Licences would issue only in extremely limited circumstances, on the reasoning that supervision fails when the substantive activity sits abroad [MAS DTSP 2025].

Relocation, not collapse

Several offshore-facing exchanges shifted staff and booking entities to Dubai and Hong Kong through the second half of 2025 [Kira estimates]. We read the outflow as concentrated in high-velocity derivative venues rather than in custody, fund servicing or issuance.

What the survivors look like

Roughly 40% of the licensed population is institution-facing — custodians, brokers and institutional desks rather than consumer apps [Kira estimates]. Bank-owned entrants continue to arrive: an institutional custodian secured a major payment institution permission in March 2025 [Hex Trust 2025].

Section 05 · Stablecoins & Settlement
009 / 018

Two settlement assets are being legislated into existence

The 2027 market clears on assets that do not yet fully exist in statute: a licensed single-currency stablecoin and a wholesale central-bank token. Both land inside the forecast window, and both favour incumbents.

The stablecoin bar is a treasury problem

Reserves must be 100% backed in cash, cash equivalents or government securities of three months or less, denominated in the peg currency, with redemption at par inside five business days [MAS SCS 2023]. The regime bites on single-currency coins pegged to the Singapore dollar or a G10 currency above a S$5 m issuance threshold [MAS SCS 2023]. Drafting for parliamentary passage was underway through 2026 [MAS wCBDC 2025].

The central-bank rail went live first

Three domestic banks settled interbank overnight lending using a Singapore dollar wholesale central-bank token in a live trial [MAS wCBDC 2025]. Tokenised bills issued to primary dealers and settled in that token were slated for 2026 [MAS wCBDC 2025] — a sovereign-grade on-chain instrument, not a pilot artefact.

The regional contrast matters

Hong Kong commenced its stablecoin ordinance in August 2025 and had granted two issuer licences by April 2026 against a HK$25 m capital floor [HKMA 2026]. Singapore trades first-mover speed for a heavier settlement stack.

Section 06 · Custody Economics
012 / 018

Safekeeping fees compress; the money moves to servicing

Basis-point custody is following the same path it took in traditional securities. The defensible margin sits adjacent — settlement, fund administration, collateral mobility and reporting against a rising compliance floor.

The fee curve is already bending

Headline safekeeping pricing has moved from roughly 55-70 basis points in 2023 to 30-45 today, and we expect 25-40 by 2027 as bank balance sheets compete for the same mandates [Kira estimates]. Winning a mandate on price alone is now a losing trade.

The cost floor is regulatory

Segregation of customer assets, a 90% cold-storage minimum, restrictions on retail lending and staking, and annual audit are mandatory, not optional service tiers [MAS PSA Custody 2023]. We put the standing compliance and technology-risk cost for a mid-sized licensee at S$2.4-3.8 m a year [Kira estimates], and enforcement has demonstrated the cost of under-investing [MAS Enf 2026].

Servicing is where the spread lives

Tokenised fund registers, on-chain settlement into central-bank money, and collateral mobility carry pricing power that raw safekeeping no longer does. Sub-scale independents without that adjacency face a structural squeeze into 2027 [Kira estimates].

Section 07 · Institutional Demand
014 / 018

The demand curve has moved from retail apps to regulated venues

Exchange-cleared derivatives, authorised tokenised funds and bank-issued on-chain paper arrived within twelve months of each other. Each converts an offshore behaviour into a supervised one.

Regulated derivatives landed first

The national exchange launched Bitcoin and Ether perpetual futures on 24 November 2025 for institutional, accredited and expert investors, cleared and margined through conventional infrastructure rather than offshore auto-liquidation [SGX 2025]. The contracts reference a product class carrying more than US$187 bn of global daily volume [SGX 2025].

Tokenised funds went retail-authorised

A tokenised US dollar short-term money market fund was authorised as a scheme and distributed through a domestic bank, with retail access from the first quarter of 2026 and a US$20 minimum [Franklin Templeton 2025]. Bank-issued tokenised bonds in S$1,000 denominations with same-day settlement arrived in January 2025 [OCBC 2025].

Capital keeps favouring the sector

Digital-asset firms rose from 5% to 16% of the domestic fintech population between 2022 and 2024 [SG Fintech 2024], and took 64% of the US$1.04 bn raised in the first half of 2025 [SG Fintech H1 2025]. Retail ownership, at 32%, is a secondary signal [IRCI 2026].
