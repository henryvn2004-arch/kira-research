How we built this view

This view is built from two evidence streams: analyst research — Lao- and English-language desk research, ministry, utility and company disclosures, and analyst synthesis — and secondary anchors — official statistics, tariff and licence documents, listed company filings and triangulated estimates. Every quantitative claim carries an explicit source tag.

Analyst research

→ Local-language desk research
Lao state media, ministry notices and utility statements — often the only place tariffs, outages and data-centre agreements are reported.
→ Analyst synthesis
KIRA in-house triangulation across multiple disclosed inputs. Tagged inline as [Kira estimates].
→ Project and agreement disclosures
Licences, feasibility studies and memoranda of understanding — read as intent until capacity is built.

Secondary anchors

→ Official and multilateral data
Statistics, central bank, World Bank and IMF releases — cited inline by named source.
→ Listed company filings
Results of listed data-centre operators and power producers, used as return and margin benchmarks.
→ Triangulated estimates
Where no single source covers a figure, we combine 2-3 inputs and disclose the method. Tagged [Kira estimates].

Conditional go: a small, anchored hall in Laos pays back; built alone at the planned tariff, payback takes ~19 years

The tension: Laos exports most of its hydropower, while Vientiane clinics still report two to three power cuts a week [AFP Sep 2026].

Years · bar = power at 4 to 10 US cents per kWh, tick = planned 7.94 cents · fill 70% (alone) or 90% (anchored)

Why the answer is conditional

Demand is real but tiny. About 4.5 MW of IT load is in use and the third-party rent pool is near US$7 m a year [Kira estimates]; rules that keep data in Laos sustain it [GDMS 2024].

Power is cheap on paper only. The ~4-cent headline [Inflect 2025] sits beside a planned 7.94 cents for industry [ANN Nov 2025], plus 2,396 distribution outages in H1 2025 [VT Aug 2025].

The anchor decides the return. A state or bank pre-let at 90% cuts payback from 19 to 8.5 years, and to 6.5 inside a partner's shell [Kira estimates].

Co-invest 1-2 MW inside a licensed Lao operator, with a state or bank anchor and a dollar power price; leave the hydro mega-campus for later.

Government and banks, not cloud tenants, make up most of the load a new operator can win

The state and its mandates sit behind the largest open segment; telcos serve their own networks.

How we sized it

Two routes agree on 3.5-6 MW. Intensity between Cambodia (0.4 W per person) and Vietnam (1.0 W) gives 3.1-7.9 MW for 7.87 m people; the segment build gives 3.4-5.7 MW [Kira estimates].

Rules create the demand. Public bodies must host in, or back up to, the National Data Center, and private firms must use approved local data centres [GDMS 2024]. At US$200 per kW a month, 3.0 MW of third-party load is ~US$7 m of rent [Kira estimates].

CaveatNo Lao operator publishes its load; treat each segment as ±30%.
Sell to the state and the banks first; a Lao hall that waits for regional cloud tenants could wait for years.

Source: [ANN Nov 2025]; [Inflect 2025]; [ERC Sep 2026]; [TNB 2025]; [EVN May 2025]; [MOIT 2025]; [KPL Sep 2026]; [Kira estimates]

Which rate an investor will pay

The 4-cent rate is not what a new user is offered. The 2025-29 plan prices processing industry at 7.94 US cents and asks zones and estates to generate their own power [ANN Nov 2025]; the ~4-cent figure is a Sep 2025 profile [Inflect 2025].

Exports set the floor. Vietnam's ceiling for new Lao hydro is 6.95 cents [MOIT 2025]: EDL earns as much abroad as at home, so a deep discount for a data centre is unlikely.

WatchEDL owes about US$4.3 bn and buys from dam owners in dollars [RFA Dec 2024]: tariffs follow the kip.
Underwrite at 7.94 cents or more, and treat any rate near 4 cents as a negotiated, dollar-denominated contract to be won, not assumed.

What it costs a data centre

Fewer, but not few. EDL logged 2,396 distribution outages and voltage drops in H1 2025, 1,107 fewer than a year earlier [VT Aug 2025]; a Vientiane clinic still reports two to three cuts a week [AFP Sep 2026].

Backup is the hidden tariff. Diesel at LAK 31,840 a litre [KPL Sep 2026] makes about US$0.37 per kWh at LAK 22,600 per dollar [ValutaFX Jul 2026]; at 3% of hours on diesel it adds ~1.1 US cents per IT kWh [Kira estimates].

Relief is datedNam Ngum 3 is due in 2027 and 680 MW of solar at 12 sites is planned [VT Aug 2025].
Design for 2N power and dual feeds from day one, and price 3-6% of hours on diesel into every quote.

How we built it

Cooling eats the advantage. At a PUE of 1.6, typical for a small tropical hall, every IT kWh needs 1.6 kWh from the meter; overheads add 5.3 cents [Kira estimates].

The comparison that matters. Delivered cost is about 14 cents in Laos against 18 in Thailand and 13 in Vietnam: a 23% edge over Thailand, a deficit to Vietnam [Kira estimates].

What would change itA dollar contract near 4 cents brings the delivered cost to about 8 cents, the only route to a real power edge.
The case rests on the power contract: without a dollar price well under 7.94 cents, Laos has no cost edge over Vietnam to sell.

017 / 039

A stand-alone Lao hall earns about 26% EBITDA, half a global operator's margin; an anchor or cheap power closes most of the gap

Margins are not like for like across models; read them as the ceiling each model can reach.

Where the gap comes from

Scale and contracts, not rent. A landlord with hyperscale leases keeps 86.8% of revenue [Keppel DC FY2025]; a global operator 49% [Equinix FY2025]. Both buy power at scale and sell to contracted tenants.

In Laos, power and fill decide it. Energy takes 51% of revenue in a 2 MW hall at 70% fill; an anchor at 90% fill lifts margin to 40%, and 4-cent power to 48% [Kira estimates].

Do not enter as a rack landlord on a public tariff; bring either an anchor contract or a power price, ideally both.

Why Laos costs more than Vietnam

Everything is imported. Generators, switchgear and cooling come in through Thailand or China with no local supply chain or contractor base [Kira estimates].

Small halls and strict licences cost more per MW. A licensed hall needs TIA-942 structures, 20 cm walls and an 800 kg per m² floor [LTP 2017]; 2N power and diesel autonomy add more for the grid [Kira estimates].

ImplicationExpanding inside an existing licensed shell cuts the cost to about US$6.5 m per MW in our model.
Prefer capacity inside a partner's licensed shell or modular pods over a greenfield building: it saves about a quarter of the capital.

What moves the answer

Rent and fill are the cliff. At US$150 per kW the hall barely covers its costs; at 50% fill payback passes 41 years [Kira estimates].

One lever alone barely clears ten years. Rent of US$250, above any Lao price we can find, gives 9.9 years; an anchor at 90% fill and US$230 gives 8.5 [Kira estimates].

ImplicationSign the tenant before the building: fill and rent are worth more than any power discount.
Make an anchor letter at US$200+ per kW and 50%+ of phase one the gate for any build spend.
