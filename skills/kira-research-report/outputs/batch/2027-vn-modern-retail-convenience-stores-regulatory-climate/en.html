Executive summary
004 / 030

Conditional go: from 18 October a Japanese-owned chain skips the needs test on every store; a Korean-owned one does not

The tension: Vietnam promised treaty investors an end to the test in January 2024, but the rule that delivers it starts only on 18 October 2026, and it does not cover every owner.

Statutory approval clock for one additional store
Vietnam · working days from a complete file to a decision · old rules vs Decree 342 by owner type
Source: [Decree 342/2026] Art. 8, 26, 27; old-rule durations [Licensing guide 2026]; sums per path [Kira estimates]

Why the answer depends on the owner

Every store still needs its own licence. A foreign-invested chain holds a retail business licence once, then a separate outlet licence from the provincial People's Committee for each store [Decree 342/2026].

The needs test now follows the owner's home country, not the store's size. Convenience stores and mini-supermarkets never qualified for the small-store exemption. From 18 October, owners from CPTPP, EVFTA and UKVFTA countries skip the test; Korean, Chinese, Thai and US owners still face it [Legal alerts Sep 2026].

Scale adds a second review. A network of 100 or more small stores sends new licences to the public security and defence ministries [Decree 342/2026].

File new stores after 18 October, put a treaty-country investor on the licence, and budget a security review once the network passes 100 stores.

Reading the rule

Location and format must both qualify. A 90 m² convenience store inside a mall still needs the test for a non-treaty owner; a 300 m² cosmetics shop in the same mall does not [DNHN Sep 2026].

The test is now local. For stores under 5,000 m², the market is the commune or ward, so the council looks at the shops on the store's own streets [Decree 342/2026].

CaveatTreaty cover depends on who counts as an investor of that country. A holding company with no real business in a treaty country may be refused the benefit; confirm with counsel.
Decide the investor of record before choosing sites: it decides whether every store after the first faces a needs test.

Why ownership shapes the race

Domestic chains expand without the foreign-investor licences. WinMart+ and Bach Hoa Xanh hold three in four of the tracked stores, and the census found almost 1,900 net new small-format stores in twelve months, most of them mini-supermarkets [Q&Me 2026].

Buying a domestic chain brings the licences back. A Vietnamese retailer that takes in foreign capital must apply for a business licence and outlet licences; it may keep trading for at most 12 months while they are issued [VCCI]; [Legal alerts Sep 2026].

An acquisition buys stores but not freedom from licensing: budget to re-license every acquired store within 12 months of closing.

Who this splits

Japanese brands gain the most. Ministop and FamilyMart run 356 stores between them, and FamilyMart plans about 300 in Hanoi by FY2031 [Q&Me 2026]; [RIN Sep 2026].

The second-largest foreign brand stays inside the test. GS25, with 318 stores, has a Korean brand owner; on our reading its partner's 70% local stake does not lift the test from its own stores [Q&Me 2026]; [Brands Vietnam GS25].

Chapter 2 · The test
013 / 030

A non-treaty store is judged against its own ward by a provincial council, on four criteria, in 20 working days

The criteria are qualitative and no score or threshold is published, so the outcome risk sits with each province.

Who decides, and how it went before

A five-department council decides. It is chaired by the provincial People's Committee and includes industry and trade, finance and the provincial police [Decree 342/2026].

Under the old rules, the wait was the problem. Aeon's head in Vietnam described needs tests causing unexplained hold-ups, sometimes taking months [RIN Sep 2026].

Wards are now bigger. Mergers cut 10,035 communes to 3,321, so a ward market now covers about three times as many shops [Gov reform 2025]; [Kira estimates].

Non-treaty chains should file each store with a ward census and a contribution case already built, so the council has nothing to ask for.

What carries over on 18 October

Existing licences stay valid. Licences issued before 18 October keep covering the activities they list [Legal alerts Sep 2026].

Pending files stay on the old rules. Applications received before 18 October are decided under Decree 09; an incomplete file has six months to be completed, after which Decree 342 applies [Legal alerts Sep 2026].

WatchA short lease silently shortens the licence, and a missed renewal stops the store trading.
Sign leases of five years or more and run a per-store renewal calendar; a store that loses its licence cannot reapply for two years.

Chapter 4 · Structure
021 / 030

GS25 already franchises to Vietnamese owners; the route trades some control for a licence clock with no council

Read for a non-treaty owner: the higher a route sits, the fewer stores wait on a needs-test council.

The franchise evidence

The model exists in Vietnam. GS25 began franchising in 2021, the first convenience brand to do so, and had 35 franchised stores by 2023 out of 211 in the south [KED Aug 2023].

It is built for small owners. Three formats of 65-70 m², 100-120 m² and 150 m², with an investment of one-third to one-fifth of other international franchise offers and a 25% expected gross margin [KED Aug 2023].

CaveatFranchisees run their own stores; format, food quality and data need contract controls a company store does not.

Prove a 30-working-day licence clock on the first ten stores before committing to a store target

Four gates for the licensing side of a roll-out, each with a test the team can measure. Budgets cover entity, counsel, licence files and permits only, not store capital [Kira estimates].

First 90 days

  1. Fix the investor of record and get counsel's written view on treaty cover.
  2. Hold every new outlet file until 18 October 2026.
  3. Build a ward-census and contribution template for needs-test files.
  4. Add a licence-linked rent-free clause to the lease template.
  5. Map food, spirits and fire permits for each store format.
  6. Start a per-store licence calendar: renewals, 15 Jan and 15 Jul reports.
