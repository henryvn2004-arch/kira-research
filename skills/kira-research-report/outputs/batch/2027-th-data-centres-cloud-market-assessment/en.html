How we built this view

This view is built from two evidence streams: analyst research — Thai- and English-language desk research, company and regulator disclosures, and analyst synthesis — and secondary anchors — investment-promotion statistics, utility and regulator statements, listed company filings, and triangulated estimates. Every quantitative claim carries an explicit source tag.

Analyst research

→ Local-language desk research
Thai trade press, bank research houses, regulator hearings and utility statements — often the only place power queues, licence changes and tariff proposals are reported.
→ Analyst synthesis
KIRA in-house triangulation across multiple disclosed inputs. Tagged inline as [Kira estimates].
→ Company & project disclosures
Investment approvals, financing closes, land sales and operator announcements — read closer to real capacity than headline pledges.

Secondary anchors

→ Statistical and promotion authorities
Board of Investment releases, energy ministry and utility statements — cited inline by named source.
→ Listed company filings
Results and releases of listed operators, power producers and estate developers — cited by company and period.
→ Triangulated estimates
Where no single source covers a figure, we combine 2-3 inputs and disclose the method. Tagged [Kira estimates].

Conditional go: Thailand is worth entering, but in Bangkok's enterprise and AI-ready colocation, not in another power-hungry EEC shell

The tension: data-centre projects have asked for about 35 GW of grid power, close to Thailand's whole peak demand, while only about 216 MW of colocation capacity is live today.

Why the answer is conditional

The domestic market is real but small. Thailand's data-centre and cloud services market reached about THB 73 bn in 2025, up from THB 20 bn in 2019 [Colliers 2025]; colocation itself is a far smaller pool [Kira estimates].

Supply is chasing regional hyperscale demand. Hyperscalers take over 80% of demand [KKPS Oct 2025], and the state now screens projects for power and water [BOI Aug 2026].

The open lane is Bangkok. Enterprise, financial and AI tenants need low latency and small, fast power blocks that the EEC megaprojects do not serve.

Enter Bangkok with a 10-30 MW AI-ready, carrier-neutral site and a Thai power or telecom partner; commit beyond phase one only with an anchor tenant.

What the count says

Facilities grew by 18 in two years [Kira estimates]. Thailand had 42 data centres in 2024, 37 of them in Bangkok [Krungsri 2025], and 60 by March 2026 [LH Bank 2026].

The national total is larger than colocation. Counting telco and enterprise rooms, capacity was about 350 MW in 2024 [TDCA 2025].

Most sites are high-grade. Over 90% serve large organisations and are built to Tier 3 or Tier 4 [LH Bank 2026].

ScaleThailand still has one MW of colocation per ~514,600 people, against ~247,700 for Asia-Pacific [C&W Sep 2026].
There is room to grow before Thailand reaches the regional density; the question is who builds it and where.

The domestic buyers

Banks and telcos are the core enterprise users, alongside IT firms [Krungsri 2025]; bank cloud use needs prior central-bank approval [Mondaq 2026].

Government is moving to cloud. The state-agency cloud budget rose to about THB 1 bn for fiscal 2025 from THB 0.2 bn [Gov Cloud 2025].

Local cloud is growing fast. INET's virtual machines rose 38% in six months to 88,842 [INET H1 2025].

Japanese baseThe JCC Bangkok surveys 1,627 member firms [JCC 2022], a ready enterprise base for Japanese-run colocation.
Plan for two customer books: a hyperscale anchor to fill halls, and an enterprise book that pays higher rent per kW and stays longer.

Retail racks earn about twice the rent per kW of a hyperscale hall, so the enterprise book carries the margin

Thai operators do not publish prices; we derive ranges from disclosed revenue and capacity and check them against regional norms.

Indicative colocation rent by price tier
USD per kW per month · Bangkok · 2025-26 · bar = range, tick = midpoint
Source: [Kira estimates] from 2024 colocation revenue [Arizton 2025] over ~100-120 MW leased, split by tier at regional premiums

How we read the range

Blended rent is about USD 120-150 per kW a month. USD 175 m of 2024 revenue [Arizton 2025] over roughly 100-120 MW in service gives that average [Kira estimates].

Power is the swing cost. Energy is about 70% of a data centre's operating budget [Nation Aug 2026], and grid power cost THB 3.95 per kWh for May-Aug 2026 [LH Bank 2026].

Yields still clear 10% unlevered on new builds, third-best in Asia-Pacific [C&W Sep 2026].

Price the hyperscale hall to fill capacity and the retail floor to earn; a mix of a third retail lifts blended rent by about a quarter [Kira estimates].

Where the queue jams

Over 90% of connection requests are in the EEC, and a 50 MW+ connection there takes 18 months or more [Mondaq 2026].

Paper demand is huge: PEA counts over 30,000 MW of applications, many speculative [PEA Aug 2026].

Upgrades cost about USD 1 bn, mainly in the EEC [MoE Sep 2026].

TariffA separate Category 9 tariff for data centres was proposed at THB 5-6 per kWh [MoE Tariff 2026], against THB 3.95 for current grid power [LH Bank 2026].
A confirmed grid date is worth more than land or capital; the scarce asset in Thailand is a megawatt with a connection letter.

016 / 037

In twelve months Thailand moved from courting data centres to rationing them, with deposits, a separate tariff and a tighter licence

Seven rule changes since October 2025; most aim at power and water, and one could cap foreign ownership of the licensed operator.

Rule changes for data centres, Oct 2025-Oct 2026
Power and investment above the line, tariffs and licences below · dates as reported
Source: [ERC DPPA 2025]; [BMI Jun 2026]; [BOI Aug 2026]; [MoE Sep 2026]; [MoE Tariff 2026]; [NBTC Jul 2026]; [ERC Sep 2026]

The one that matters for foreigners

Telecom licence Type 3 needs 75% Thai shareholding, and BOI promotion cannot override it [Mondaq 2026].

NBTC counts 26 licensed data-centre operators: 23 on Type 1, 3 on Type 3, and proposes moving the sector to Type 3 in 2026 [NBTC Jul 2026].

The BOI now asks for a THB 4.5 m deposit per MW and screens power, water, technology and jobs [BOI Aug 2026].

WatchIf the Type 3 shift applies to colocation, a foreign operator needs a Thai-majority licensed partner.
Structure the entry so that a Thai-majority partner can hold the licence if Type 3 applies; do not assume today's Type 1 regime lasts.

What separates open from crowded

Bangkok holds two-thirds of live MW [BMI Jun 2026] and gets small blocks connected faster than the EEC queue [Mondaq 2026].

First 90 days

  1. Shortlist three Thai power producers or telcos with connected capacity in Bangkok or Samut Prakan.
  2. Get a legal opinion on NBTC Type 1 versus Type 3 for colocation and design a structure that works under both.
  3. Ask MEA and PEA for indicative connection dates for 10, 20 and 40 MW.
  4. Model the BOI tier: PUE ≤ 1.3 and GPU capability for the longest tax holiday.
  5. Open talks with AI cloud providers and Japanese and Korean enterprise users for pre-lets.
  6. Map supply contracts open in the EEC builds: cooling, switchgear, renewable power.
