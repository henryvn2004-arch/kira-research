How we built this view

This view is built from two evidence streams: primary inputs — operator and licensing-office reads, contract research desk conversations, and analyst synthesis — and secondary anchors — agency statistics, statutory instruments, and company disclosures. Every quantitative claim carries an explicit source tag.

Primary inputs

→ Contract research desk reads
Conversations with clinical operations and business development staff on study start-up timelines, site competition, pricing pressure, and where regional mandates are actually booked.
→ Technology transfer reads
Reads from university and agency licensing offices on deal structure, timelines to executed terms, equity-versus-royalty preference, and what stalls an asset before partnering.
→ Analyst synthesis
KIRA in-house triangulation across multiple disclosed inputs where no single source covers the figure. Tagged inline as [Kira estimates], with the method disclosed at the point of use.

Secondary anchors

→ Agency statistics & policy
Manufacturing performance releases, investment commitment reviews, the national research plan, medicines regulator guidance, and trade statistics — cited inline by named source and period.
→ Operator disclosures
Site investment, capacity and construction announcements; licensing and partnering releases; annual reports from national drug-discovery and precision-medicine platforms.
→ Triangulated estimates
Where a figure is unpublished — installed bioreactor capacity, services revenue booked locally, trial cost indices, deal counts — we combine inputs and disclose the method. Tagged [Kira estimates].

Singapore biomedical — capacity is arriving faster than deals

A disclosed 144,000-litre build-out lands into a tariff regime that reprices where a molecule is finished, while the research base converts into licensable assets at a fraction of the regional deal cadence. Capacity is the visible half

The biomedical manufacturing cluster grew 16.2% year on year across the first ten months of 2025, with the pharmaceuticals segment up 122.9% in October alone on higher active-ingredient and biological output [EDB Mfg 2025]. Against a base of S$38.1 bn in 2023 [EDB BMS 2023], we put 2025 output near S$43 bn and 2027 near S$49 bn [Kira estimates]. Disclosed additions alone bring 144,000 litres of drug-substance capacity on line between 2024 and 2027 [WuXi Bio 2026] [AbbVie 2024].

Origination is the harder half

The national drug-discovery platform has executed close to 20 licensing deals across 25 corporate partners and more than 70 academic institutions [EDDC AR 2025]. We count eight Singapore-origin out-licensing transactions in 2025 against three in 2022 [Kira estimates] — genuine momentum, set against US$135.7 bn of Chinese out-licensing value in the same year [China Lic 2026].

Five strategic implications for market participants

The 2027 window rewards operators who separate what Singapore can win — regulatory speed, data quality and originated intellectual property — from what it has already lost, which is the cost argument.

01
Route the molecule, not the plant
Branded finished product entering the United States carries a 100% duty unless onshoring conditions are met, while generics and active ingredients stay at the zero reciprocal rate [US Sec 232 2026]. Book drug substance and active-ingredient steps here; place US-bound finished dose against the exemption test.
ANCHOR: 31 Jul 2026 first effective date [US Sec 232 2026]
02
Sell the regulatory clock, not the rate card
Trial authorisation runs about 30 working days, bioequivalence studies about 15, and ethics review proceeds in parallel [HSA CT 2025]. We index a Phase III oncology study here at roughly 72 against North America at 100 [Kira estimates] — uncompetitive on price, decisive on time to first patient.
ANCHOR: 1 Apr 2025 ethics mutual recognition live [HSA CT 2025]
03
Treat the licence-out as the exit
A domestic antibody-drug conjugate platform was licensed worldwide and spun into a company that raised a US$187 m Series A [Hummingbird 2025]. Close to 20 deals have come off the national discovery platform [EDDC AR 2025]. Build the partnering package early; the listing is not the plan.
ANCHOR: ~20 deals cumulative [EDDC AR 2025]
04
Price the regional comparison into every term sheet
Chinese assets took roughly half of global licence-out value in 2025 on US$135.7 bn of deals, with average upfronts rising to US$172 m [China Lic 2026]. A Singapore asset is bought for differentiation and data package quality, never for volume or discount.
ANCHOR: US$60 bn Chinese deals in Q1 2026 alone [China Lic 2026]
05
Anchor on the population asset
The precision-medicine programme entered its final phase in November 2025, enrolling about 10% of the resident population over five years to 2031 [NPM Phase III 2025], on top of a completed 100,000-genome cohort. Multi-ancestral Asian depth is the one input regional rivals cannot replicate.
ANCHOR: 100,000 genomes already sequenced [NPM Phase III 2025]

Section 05 · Cluster Composition
007 / 019

A narrow base, carrying a wide share of the economy

Biomedical output is concentrated in a handful of plants on one estate, yet it carries roughly a fifth of manufacturing value-added and the fifth-largest export line. Concentration cuts both ways — it delivers scale economics and it transmits any single policy shock straight through.

Weight out of proportion to footprint

Biomedical manufacturing generated S$38.1 bn of output in 2023 and supplied about 22% of total manufacturing value-added [EDB BMS 2023], on roughly 2.6% of gross domestic product [EDB BMS 2023]. Pharmaceutical products were the fifth-largest export line in 2024 at US$15.5 bn, ranking Singapore sixteenth among exporters worldwide [OEC Trade 2024].

The mix is tilting to biologics

We split the 2023 cluster at roughly S$27.0 bn pharmaceuticals and biologics against S$11.1 bn medical technology, moving to S$36.5 bn and S$12.5 bn by 2027 [Kira estimates]. The growth is disproportionately biologic: the October 2025 print showed the pharmaceuticals segment up 122.9% on higher active-ingredient and biological production [EDB Mfg 2025].

Commitment has not slowed

Investment commitments in 2025 reached S$14.2 bn of fixed assets, of which manufacturing took about S$12.1 bn, alongside S$8.9 bn of annual business spending and 15,700 jobs [EDB Review 2025]. Four companies joined the precision-medicine pre-competitive consortium in the same year [EDB Review 2025].

Section 05 · Tariff Geography
009 / 019

The duty attaches to the product, not the plant

The 2026 pharmaceutical tariff order splits the export book cleanly. Branded finished dose carries the full rate; active ingredients and generics do not. The exposed slice is narrower than the headline, but it sits on the highest-margin line.

A tiered structure with two dates

Patented and branded pharmaceutical imports into the United States carry a 100% duty unless onshoring conditions are met, while many generic medicines and active pharmaceutical ingredients continue to enter at the zero reciprocal rate [US Sec 232 2026]. The rate takes effect on 31 July 2026 for seventeen named companies and on 29 September 2026 for everyone else [US Sec 232 2026].

Sizing the exposure honestly

Singapore shipped US$15.5 bn of pharmaceutical products in 2024, its fifth-largest export line [OEC Trade 2024]. We put the United States at roughly 12-16% of that value, and branded finished dose at well under half of the US-bound share [Kira estimates]. Sites weighted to active ingredients and drug substance face limited direct impact.

Capital has not blinked

Investment commitments still reached S$14.2 bn of fixed assets in 2025, with manufacturing taking about S$12.1 bn and 15,700 jobs attached [EDB Review 2025]. Decisions of this size are taken on a decade view; none of the disclosed build-out has been withdrawn.

Section 06 · Trial Economics
012 / 019

Priced out on cost, competitive on the clock

On a per-patient basis Singapore sits near the top of the regional cost table and will stay there. What it sells instead is elapsed time — a short authorisation window, parallel ethics review, and mutual recognition across the major review boards.

The cost argument is lost, deliberately

A Phase III oncology study in the lowest-cost regional markets can run up to 60% below North American levels [APAC Trials 2026]. We index the same study here at roughly 72 against North America at 100, above Australia at about 70 and Korea at about 65 [Kira estimates]. No pricing strategy closes that gap.

The clock is the product

Trial authorisation runs about 30 working days, Phase I bioequivalence studies about 15, and ethics review proceeds in parallel rather than in sequence [HSA CT 2025]. Since 1 April 2025 a mutual recognition agreement between five major review boards lets one board clear a multi-centre study for all sites [HSA CT 2025].

Where that wins the mandate

Sponsors increasingly select the region for regulatory speed, population diversity and digitally mature sites rather than for cost alone [APAC Trials 2026]. That reframing is what keeps a high-cost market in the site list — as the first-patient site, not the volume site.

Section 07 · Translation Funnel
014 / 019

A wide research base narrowing to a handful of partnered assets

Every stage of the funnel is well instrumented until the last two. The constraint is not discovery or company formation — it is the step from a funded company to an asset a global buyer will pay an upfront for.

The input side is well funded

The national research plan commits S$37 bn over five years, a 32% increase on the S$28 bn predecessor and about 1% of gross domestic product [RIE2030 Plan]. S$10.8 bn — 29% of the plan — sits across four domains including human health [RIE2030 Plan]. Research spending ran at 2.03% of output in 2023 [RIE Survey 2023], with business expenditure up 11.9% across the preceding year [RIE Survey 2023].

The transfer machinery has been re-plumbed

A common licensing framework across the two largest universities cut execution from up to five months to one month [NUS-NTU GRIP]. Their combined venture programmes have incubated over 400 teams and produced close to 160 spin-offs [NUS-NTU GRIP]. The two state investment arms merged on 1 April 2025, with the co-investment cap lifted from S$8 m to S$12 m [SG Growth Capital 2025].

The output is thin but compounding

The dedicated biotechnology company population rose roughly fourfold, from about 12 in 2015 to more than 60 in 2025 [A*STAR 2025]. Two originated therapeutic assets are in clinical development off the national platform [EDDC AR 2025].

Section 07 · Deal Cadence
015 / 019

Deal count is rising against a regional benchmark that moved first

Singapore-origin assets are being licensed on real terms — worldwide exclusivity, upfronts, milestones and royalties. The difficulty is scale of comparison: the buyer evaluating a local asset has just spent a record year buying Chinese ones.

The named deals are real

In January 2025 a domestic biotherapeutics house granted a US oncology developer an exclusive worldwide licence to monoclonal antibodies against an undisclosed target, for an upfront plus development and commercial milestones and royalties [Hummingbird 2025]. In March 2025 the same house licensed its multipayload antibody-drug conjugate platform worldwide for equity, milestones and royalties, spinning it into a company that raised a US$187 m Series A [Hummingbird 2025].

The cadence is the constraint

We count eight Singapore-origin out-licensing transactions in 2025 against three in 2022, and put 2027 near 14 on the current pipeline [Kira estimates]. An agency spin-out backed by corporate venture and the state investor in-licensed a partner's technology for T-cell receptor immunotherapy in the same period [ImmunoScape 2025] — the flow runs both ways.

The comparison a buyer actually makes

Chinese out-licensing reached US$135.7 bn in 2025, nearly triple the US$51.9 bn of 2024, and took roughly half of global licence-out deal value against 27-28% for the United States [China Lic 2026]. Average upfronts rose from US$52 m in 2022 to US$172 m [China Lic 2026]. A local asset wins on differentiation, never on volume.
