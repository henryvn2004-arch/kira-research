How we built this view

This view is built from two evidence streams: analyst research — local-language desk research, company and channel disclosures, and analyst synthesis — and secondary anchors — statistical authorities, listed company filings, and triangulated estimates. Every quantitative claim carries an explicit source tag.

Analyst research

→ Local-language desk research
Trade press, regulator notices and industry-body releases in the market's own language — often the only place local prices, store counts and rule changes are reported.
→ Analyst synthesis
KIRA in-house triangulation across multiple disclosed inputs. Tagged inline as [Kira estimates].
→ Company & channel disclosures
Store-network announcements, distributor and platform disclosures, retailer price listings — read closer to the demand signal than headline data.

Secondary anchors

→ Statistical authorities
Government statistical offices, central banks, customs data — cited inline by named source.
→ Listed company filings
Annual reports, prospectuses, investor decks of named players — cited inline by company + period.
→ Triangulated estimates
Where no single source covers a figure, we combine 2-3 inputs and disclose the method. Tagged [Kira estimates].

Conditional go: the white space is the northern ring around Hanoi, not another Ho Chi Minh City network

The tension: outside Vietnam's two big cities, about 80 million people share fewer than 200 chain convenience stores, yet the chains that came first are still losing money.

Why the answer is conditional

The south is taken and still unprofitable. Two in three chain convenience stores sit in Ho Chi Minh City [Q&Me 2026], and GS25 lost VND 125 bn in 2024 on about VND 2 trn of sales [Znews Apr 2025].

Hanoi is opening, but its core is contested. GS25, 7-Eleven and FamilyMart all entered between 2025 and 2026, against a Circle K base of 191 stores [Q&Me 2026].

The open ground is the northern ring (Hanoi townships, Hai Phong, Quang Ninh, Bac Ninh): rising incomes, almost no chain stores, and Decree 342 removes the needs test for Japanese investors from 18 October 2026 [Decree 342/2026].

Enter as a food-led cluster in the north, structured for Decree 342, and commit beyond pilot only when stores hold VND 30 m a day.

Why the mix is about to move faster

Four in five grocery dong still go through markets and family shops. Traditional trade fell from 89% to 80% between 2010 and 2024 [SHS Dec 2025]; general trade still carries up to 81% of FMCG sales [B-Company May 2026].

The 2026 tax switch thins the shop base. Lump-sum tax ended on 1 Jan 2026; if 15-20% of ~1.4 m shops close or merge over three years, 200,000-280,000 points of sale disappear [B-Company May 2026].

Fresh food stays in the wet market. 85% of shoppers buy meat and seafood only there (survey via [SHS Dec 2025]), so the shift reaches packaged and ready-to-eat goods first.

ImplicationThe shoppers who leave family shops buy packaged goods and meals near home: exactly a proximity store's basket.
Plan sites where family shops are closing fastest: urban wards and new townships, where formal shoppers already outnumber shops.

A concentrated, foreign-led channel

Five foreign brands hold 91% of chain stores (1,322 of ~1,450) [Kira estimates from Q&Me 2026]. Circle K alone held 48% of channel revenue at end-2023 [VNS Jan 2025].

Growth now comes from the challengers: GS25 +82, FamilyMart +32 and 7-Eleven +18 in the year to March 2026, while Circle K sat at ~500 and Ministop slipped from 197 to 184 [Q&Me 2026].

CaveatCensus and company counts differ: GS25 reported 385 stores in Oct 2025 [MT Oct 2025] and 400+ in 2026 [DNHN Jul 2026].
Expect the leaders to defend their cities with density, not price; a newcomer gains more from places they are not yet in.

How we built it

Sales of VND 24-26 m a day are the network norm. GS25 (2024), 7-Eleven (2023) and Circle K (2022) all divide to VND 8.8-9.4 bn a store a year [Kira estimates].

Two fixed costs sink the store: rent and round-the-clock staff take 19% of sales before utilities. Gross margin of 20-25% [VNS Jan 2025] cannot carry both.

CaveatThroughput is inferred from chain totals; a pilot store's till data replaces this model.
Three levers fix it: sales above VND 30 m a day, rent under 10% of sales, and a food mix that lifts gross margin toward 30%.

What the rent bands mean

Rent is the swing factor. Average ground-floor rents run USD 53-58 /m² in the two cities [Savills H1 2025] [CBRE HCMC 2025]; Hanoi prime streets ask USD 120-180 [B-Company Hanoi 2026].

Provincial and township rents are a third of that. Da Nang retail asks ~USD 30 /m² [CBRE Da Nang Q1 2026].

Decision ruleReject any site whose expected sales sit below its rent band's line. A food mix at ~30% gross margin lowers every bar by about a fifth [Kira estimates].
Hunt for sites, not cities: a township shophouse at USD 25-35 /m² with VND 30 m a day beats a central corner at any realistic volume.

Why the middle format

Food lifts both levers at once. Ready meals carry higher margins than packaged goods and create lunch and evening visits a minimart does not serve.

The evidence exists: GS25's best sellers in Hanoi were its own-label items and Korean meals [Sateconomy Apr 2025]; Circle K's cooked food and seating set it apart.

CaveatNo published basket or margin data split by category; the 28-32% margin is our target, to be proven in the pilot.
Design the pilot store around hot food and seating first, packaged goods second, and keep fresh produce out.
