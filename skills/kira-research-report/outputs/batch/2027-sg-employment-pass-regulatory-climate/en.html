How we built this view

This brief reads the Employment Pass rules a bank must clear, then tests what the 2027 changes mean for the people a foreign bank actually sponsors. It rests on two evidence streams: primary rule texts (Ministry of Manpower tables, parliamentary replies, budget factsheets) and market evidence (workforce statistics, salary distributions, company disclosures, advisory alerts). Every quantitative claim carries an explicit source tag.

Rules and their timing

→ Official tables
The EP qualifying salary table by age (current and 2027), the COMPASS criteria and exemptions, the job-advertising rules, read from Ministry of Manpower pages updated in 2026.
→ Benchmark releases
The August 2026 COMPASS salary benchmarks and the August 2025 table they replace, with effective dates checked against several advisory alerts.
→ Our reading, flagged
Where a rule leaves room for judgement (exemptions, flexibilities), we say how we read it. This brief is market research, not immigration or legal advice.

Market evidence

→ Workforce and pay data
Pass-holder counts to June 2026, salary percentiles of new EP applications to 2025, resident income and PMET growth, financial-sector workforce data.
→ Company and local-language sources
Disclosures and press on Japanese and Korean banks in Singapore, searched in Japanese, Korean and Chinese as well as English.
→ Triangulated estimates
Applicant archetype scores, the cost of each gate and the exposure map combine published thresholds with stated assumptions. Tagged [Kira estimates].

The floor is only the first gate

An EP candidate must clear an age-linked salary floor and then score 40 points on a test that weighs pay against local peers, the degree, and the firm's own nationality mix. For a bank staffed from its home country, the second gate is the harder one.

What this means for a foreign bank

C1 and C3 move against the parent's own nationals. A Japanese hire into a branch where Japanese staff are 25% or more of PMETs scores 0 on diversity, so salary and degree must carry 40 points between them [MOM EP 2026].

C4 rewards local hiring at firm level. Each Singaporean or PR professional hired raises the local share that every future application is scored on.

The bonus routes are thin in finance. The 2026 shortage list names one finance role, advisers to ultra-high-net-worth clients and family offices [SOL 2026 alerts].

WatchC1 compares pay with local peers in the same sector and age band, so a bank is benchmarked against bank salaries, not the national average.
Before the next renewal round, compute the firm's nationality shares and local PMET share; they change every score at once.

Reading the calendar

Renewals key off the expiry date, not the filing date. A pass expiring on 2 July 2027 is scored on the August 2026 benchmarks even if the renewal is filed in May [EY Aug 2026].

The benchmarks move every year. MOM reviews them annually from labour force survey data, so a holder who passes in 2027 can fail in 2028 at the same pay [KPMG Aug 2026].

The floor moves every two years or so. Increases took effect in 2022, 2025 and 2027 [MOM COS 2024; MOM EP 2026].

Already in forceThe 2026 renewal floor (S$6,200 in finance) and the 2026 Shortage Occupation List apply to every filing today.
Sort the EP register by expiry date into these five windows; the July-December 2027 window is the first to need action.

What the table says

The rise is 6.5% at 23 and 7.6% at 45. A 40-year-old finance candidate needs S$11,314 from 2027, up S$787 [MOM EP 2026].

Finance pays a premium to qualify. At 45 the finance floor is S$12,700 against S$11,500 elsewhere, because MOM benchmarks it to higher sector wage norms [MOM COS 2024].

The cost is small per head. Lifting a holder from the current to the 2027 floor at 40 costs about S$9,400 a year, before any bonus effect [Kira estimates].

Budget the floor increase as a known cost; the decision money belongs to the COMPASS gap, which is several times larger.

Why age is the lever

The floor rises with age; applicant pay is pooled. At 35 the 2027 floor is S$9,927, above the 2025 lower quartile of S$9,700; under today's table the crossing is at 37 [MOM EP 2026; MOM PQ Jan 2026].

The median is still safe. At 45 the 2027 floor of S$12,700 is 85% of the S$15,000 median, so a typical senior applicant clears it.

The upper quartile sits at the exemption line. At S$22,100, about a quarter of finance applicants are close to the S$22,500 COMPASS exemption [Kira estimates].

Flag every holder aged 35 or more paid under about S$12,000; that is where the floor, not the points test, ends the case.

What moved in August 2026

Banking at 40 rose 2.2% for 10 points and 1.7% for 20 points, to S$16,955 and S$26,783 [EIG Aug 2026].

Across sectors the 10-point line rose about 3.6% and the 20-point line about 2.1%, on an unweighted average; insurance rose faster than average [KPMG Aug 2026].

How we scored it

Age raises every row. Both the floor and the banking benchmark climb with age faster than typical pay for mid-office roles.

022 / 033

Keeping a 40-year-old home-country hire costs S$787 a month to clear the floor, but S$6,400 a month to earn the first salary points

The price ladder for one finance EP holder aged 40, starting from pay at today's floor. Each step is a separate route, not a cumulative plan.

Reading the ladder

The floor step is cheap. S$787 a month, or about S$9,400 a year, keeps the holder eligible from 2027 [MOM EP 2026; Kira estimates].

The points step is not. Reaching the banking 10-point line of S$16,955 means a 61% rise from today's floor pay [EIG Aug 2026].

Past that, exemption is close. Another S$5,545 reaches S$22,500, where COMPASS no longer applies; for a holder already near S$17,000 this can be the cleaner choice.

AlternativeTransferring the holder from a group entity abroad removes the points step and leaves only the floor.

Where the doubt is

Nationality mix is the weakest condition. Japanese and Korean branches staffed from home often sit above the 25% line that zeroes C3; a change needs years of hiring, not one cycle.

The benchmarks will move again. MOM updates them every August, so pay bands set in 2027 need review in 2028 [KPMG Aug 2026].

Flexibilities are not a plan. MOM grants targeted exceptions for economic priorities but publishes no criteria or counts [MOM PQ Jan 2026].

Fix the register first; every other condition is measured from it.
