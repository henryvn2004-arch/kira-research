How we built this view

This view is built from two evidence streams: analyst research — local-language desk research, company and channel disclosures, and analyst synthesis — and secondary anchors — statistical authorities, listed company filings, and triangulated estimates. Every quantitative claim carries an explicit source tag.

Analyst research

→ Local-language desk research
Trade press, regulator notices and industry-body releases in the market's own language — often the only place local prices, store counts and rule changes are reported.
→ Analyst synthesis
KIRA in-house triangulation across multiple disclosed inputs. Tagged inline as [Kira estimates].
→ Company & channel disclosures
Store-network announcements, distributor and platform disclosures, retailer price listings — read closer to the demand signal than headline data.

Secondary anchors

→ Statistical authorities
Government statistical offices, central banks, customs data — cited inline by named source.
→ Listed company filings
Annual reports, prospectuses, investor decks of named players — cited inline by company + period.
→ Triangulated estimates
Where no single source covers a figure, we combine 2-3 inputs and disclose the method. Tagged [Kira estimates].

01 · THE PRIZE IS REAL
Chains are taking share from a shrinking field. Coffee chains grew 27% and tea chains 28% in 2025 [MW 2026] while total F&B revenue grew about 5% [Kira estimates]. A 20-store network is worth about USD 7m a year in sales [Kira estimates].
02 · BUT NOT ON PRICE
The budget box is full. Highlands serves the everyday chain visit at VND 47k a guest [Q&Me 2026] across 947 Vietnamese outlets [JFC 2025]; Milano and E-Coffee add over 3,300 low-price kiosks and shops [MW 2026] [Trung Nguyen 2025]. A Korean budget-coffee import would fight them head on.
03 · THE GAP IS THE OCCASION
Food and seats are under-served. 77% of chain visits are drink-only, yet adding food lifts the ticket from VND 88k to 162k [Q&Me 2026]. No measured chain sells a tea-led visit above VND 60k a guest. A bakery-café concept fits that gap.
Enter through a joint venture with a partner that controls sites; open three pilot stores and scale only if they hold VND 22m a day for six months.

Where the stores went

Outlets grew from 816 to 2,067 between 2019 and 2025 [b-company 2025]. Ho Chi Minh City still holds 43% of them, Hanoi 21% [Kira estimates].

The provinces grew fastest, 4.5x. Chains followed income growth into second-tier cities; urban incomes averaged VND 7.4m a month in 2025 against VND 5.2m in rural areas [GSO 2025].

ImplicationA first wave belongs in the two big cities, where seats, office workers and delivery density are; provinces suit a later franchise wave.
Pilot in Ho Chi Minh City, then Hanoi; hold provinces for franchisees once the format is proven.

Networks are local

Starbucks holds about 3% of these outlets after 13 years in the market [Kira estimates]; it reached 150 stores only in January 2026 [Tuoi Tre 2026].

Scale sits in two models: franchised low-price kiosks (Milano, E-Coffee) and company-run cafés (Highlands opened 134 stores in 2025 alone [JFC 2025]).

Foreign chains entered on hype and retreated on store economics

Picture a Dong Khoi flagship with a queue round the block in 2014; a few years later the brand's outlets had moved to food courts. The same arc has repeated since.

Foreign entries and local pivots, coffee chains
Vietnam · 2013-2026 · axis broken between 2016 and 2022

The pattern

Queues at launch did not last. Caffe Bene cut its 300-store goal to 100 within a year [Vietstock 2019]; Tous les Jours had 37 stores in 2023 [WCP 2024] against 100 planned for 2020 [Inside Retail 2017].

Even strong locals retreat when a format fails: Phuc Long shut its supermarket kiosks in late 2023 at a VND 42bn cost [Znews 2024].

What would change itA format proven store by store before the network is built; none of the stalled entrants did that.
Treat opening-week queues as noise; commit capital only on six-month store sales.

Section 07 · Store economics

A reference store keeps about a fifth of sales after rent, staff and the delivery apps

This is our reference P&L for the entrant concept, built from published cost inputs; it is a threshold to test, not a forecast.

Reference store assumptions

A 120 m² café at VND 25m a day sells VND 750m a month; staff cost assumes 14 people at VND 8m fully loaded, about 1.5x the region-I minimum wage [Decree 293/2025] [Kira estimates].

Rent is budgeted at VND 120m a month, about twice the average District 1 street-front asking rent of VND 62m [VOH 2025], for a corner or two-frontage site; prime Dong Khoi ground floors at USD 80-150 per m² [b-company 2026] do not fit this model.

Delivery30% of sales through apps at a 22% effective fee; platforms charge 20-25% on average and 25-30% to newcomers [Vietnam News 2025].

Why food is the lever

Ten more points of food attachment add VND 1.7m a day, as much as 18 extra tickets, with no new seat, rent or staff [Kira estimates].

Traffic is the expensive lever: extra tickets need marketing, delivery spend or a better site; a pastry case needs an oven and a recipe.

Why the JV

The JV is the only route fair or better on every criterion. It trades some speed for control of the brand and the recipes, and borrows the partner's site pipeline.
