How we built this view

This scan asks where a new charging operator or equipment maker can earn a return in Indonesia, not only how fast the market grows. It rests on two evidence streams: official and company data (ministry and PLN counts, sales statistics, fleet disclosures) and charger economics built from the published tariff caps. Every quantitative claim carries an explicit source tag.

Data we used

→ Charger counts and the state plan
Public charging (SPKLU) and battery-swap (SPBKLU) counts from the energy ministry and PLN, the 2025-2030 charging plan, the tariff and fee decisions, and PLN's province map of charging sites.
→ Vehicle sales and fleet data
Car wholesales by powertrain and brand, motorcycle and e-scooter sales, the EV population reported by the industry ministry, and ride-hailing and taxi fleet disclosures.
→ Local-language press
Indonesian-language coverage of charging use, holiday peaks, tariffs and operators, where most charger and fleet numbers are first reported.

How we analysed it

→ Charger economics, with the method shown
Energy sold per charger, the tariff and fee stack, and payback by site type combine disclosed inputs with stated assumptions. Tagged [Kira estimates].
→ Segment and region scoring
Each segment and region is rated on how attractive it is and how winnable it is for a foreign operator or equipment maker; the ratings are our judgement from the evidence.
→ Company mapping
Charger makers, operators, swap networks and fleet anchors are listed from company and press disclosures; no company is included without a source.

Conditional go: the return is in fleet charging hubs around Jakarta, not in the next 5,000 roadside chargers

The tension: battery-electric car sales doubled in 2026 while the public network grew 2% in five months, yet the average charger still sells a sixth of what it needs to pay back.

Why the answer is conditional

Demand is real and concentrated. Battery-electric cars took 16.9% of car wholesales in January-August 2026, up from 12.9% in 2025 [detikOto Sep 2026] [Kumparan Jan 2026], and about 63% of electric cars are in Jakarta [Bapenda DKI Jul 2026].

Owners charge at home, so public units sit idle. A PLN unit in West Java sold about 52 kWh a day in 2025; an ultra-fast unit needs about 300 to pay back in five years [Kira estimates].

Fleets cannot charge at home. Grab ran 28,000 EVs in June 2026 and plans to triple them by December [Bisnis Jun 2026]: contracted depot volume is the one load that fills a charger.

Enter as a fleet-hub operator in Greater Jakarta with an anchor contract, sell hardware into the state build-out, and leave outer islands and scooter swapping for later.

A fast build that ran out of road

The network grew five-fold in two years. From 911 units in 2023 to 4,778 at 3,093 locations by December 2025 [ESDM 2023 data] [ESDM Feb 2026], led by PLN and its partners.

Then it stopped. May 2026 showed 4,892 units [Jawa Pos May 2026]. The plan calls for 9,633 in 2026, 62,918 by 2030 and about 192,000 by 2034 [Kepmen ESDM 24.K/2025].

Permits are no longer the brake. Since February 2026 an operator needs only a business number (NIB), with no supply-plan requirement [CNN Indonesia Feb 2026].

ImplicationThe constraint is the return per charger, not paperwork. That is what the rest of this report tests.
The plan creates room for about 4,700 units in 2026 alone; who fills it depends on utilisation, not on permits.

Who is left for public chargers

Public units sell trips and top-ups. Homecoming journeys, intercity runs and drivers caught short; that demand is real but seasonal and thin.

The steady users are people without a garage. Apartment owners and, above all, ride-hail and taxi drivers who drive 200 km or more a day and cannot wait overnight.

What would change itIf Jakarta ends the tax perk, fewer second cars; if apartments add chargers, more public-style demand moves into buildings.
Build for drivers who cannot charge at home; a public network aimed at private owners competes with PLN's own night tariff.

How we built the base case

Capex: PLN budgeted Rp72.84 bn for 60 ultra-fast 200 kW units, about Rp1.2 bn each [PLN UFC capex 2022]; press estimates put an ultra-fast unit near Rp1 bn [detikOto 2023 capex].

Net cash per kWh: Rp1,761 energy margin over the bulk tariff, plus a session fee of about half the cap (~Rp1,140 per kWh), less ~25% for site rent, upkeep and payments [Kira estimates].

Use today: PLN's 501 West Java units sold 9.48 GWh in 2025, about 52 kWh each a day [RRI Jan 2026].

CaveatCapex dates from 2022-23 and hardware prices have fallen; at Rp0.8 bn the threshold drops to about 200 kWh a day.
Underwrite a charger on contracted kWh per day, not on the car count around it.

What one kWh earns at a public ultra-fast unit
Indonesia · Rp per kWh · regulated caps · fee spread over an average 23.6 kWh session
Source: [ESDM SPKLU tariff]; [Kepmen ESDM 182.K/2023]; session size from [PLN Lebaran 2026]; per-kWh fee [Kira estimates]

The fee, not the energy, is the swing

The energy margin is fixed by two numbers. PLN sells bulk power to operators at Rp714 per kWh and drivers pay at most Rp2,475 [ESDM SPKLU tariff].

The session fee is capped per session, not per kWh. Up to Rp57,000 ultra-fast and Rp25,000 fast [Kepmen ESDM 182.K/2023]; spread over a 23.6 kWh session it can double the take, but it is what price competition attacks first.

WatchThe fee caps are reviewed every two years; the bulk tariff is an incentive, not a law.
A business plan that needs the full session fee to work will not survive the first price war with PLN.

What separates the rows

Fleet depots win on volume, not price. We assume fleets pay the energy-only price with no session fee (net ~Rp1,200 per kWh) and still pay back in 3-7 years.

Cheap AC units pay back fast but earn little: about Rp20 m a year each, a hotel or mall amenity rather than a business.

Rest areas and malls need volume they do not have outside holidays; outer-island towns do not pay back on any volume we can observe.

A newcomer's first capital belongs in fleet depots; rest areas only with a land partner, and outer islands not at all.

At the capped public energy price an EV runs at under half the fuel cost per km; the full ultra-fast fee erases most of the saving

For a private owner charging at home this is a footnote; for a driver covering 200 km a day it is the business case.

Energy cost per km driven

Source: [Investortrust Oct 2026] (Pertalite); [ESDM SPKLU tariff]; [Kepmen ESDM 182.K/2023]; efficiency and per-km maths [Kira estimates]

Why fleets will pay for a depot

The saving is the driver's income. At 200 km a day, energy-only public charging saves about Rp90,000 a day against petrol; the full ultra-fast fee takes most of that back [Kira estimates].

A depot at bulk power is the floor. An operator that buys at Rp714 and sells to a fleet below the public price still earns a margin, and the fleet still saves.

ImplicationFleet contracts will be priced near energy-only. The margin comes from volume and from opening idle bays to the public.
Sell fleets energy-only depot charging below the public price; it is the offer PLN's public network cannot match.

Our recommendation

Operators: build-own-operate depots for one or two fleets in Greater Jakarta, priced below the public energy price, with idle bays opened to the public.

First 90 days

  1. Shortlist three fleets (ride-hail, taxi, logistics) and ask for volume letters.
  2. Ask PLN for connection quotes and dates at three Jabodetabek sites.
  3. Get installed-cost quotes for 200 kW units from three suppliers.
  4. Set the fleet price below Rp2,475 per kWh with no session fee.
  5. Register the operating company (NIB) and check PLN's partner terms.
  6. Build a per-unit dashboard: kWh a day, uptime, queue time.
