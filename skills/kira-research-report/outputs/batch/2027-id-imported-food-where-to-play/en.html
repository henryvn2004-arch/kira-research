How we built this view

This view rests on two evidence streams: analyst research in English, Bahasa Indonesia, Japanese and Korean, and secondary anchors from trade statistics, regulators and listed companies. Every quantitative claim carries a source tag. Market size is measured as the import value of consumer-oriented food; retail value is higher.

Analyst research

→ Local-language desk research
Bahasa Indonesia press, regulator notices and company pages, often the only place licence delays, store openings and halal rules are reported; Japanese and Korean sources for exporter data.
→ Analyst synthesis
KIRA in-house triangulation across disclosed inputs, tagged inline as [Kira estimates], with the method shown on the page.
→ Company and regulator disclosures
Listed-company results, retailer releases, halal agency regulations and statements, read for dates and scope.

Secondary anchors

→ Trade statistics
Consumer-oriented import series by category (Trade Data Monitor via the US agricultural attaché in Jakarta), Japanese export statistics, Korean export data.
→ Official anchors
Statistics Indonesia (BPS), Bank Indonesia, the halal agency (BPJPH) and food agency (BPOM), trade ministry rules.
→ Triangulated estimates
Where no single source covers a figure (price build-up, 2030 cases, segment scores) we combine 2-3 inputs and show the logic. Values are import values (CIF), not retail sales, unless stated.

Conditional go: imported food is a US$9 bn pool still growing, but only where Indonesia cannot make the product itself

The tension: imports rose 22% in four years, yet in every category that reached scale, the brands built local plants and the imports faded.

Why the answer is conditional

The pool is real. Consumer food imports reached US$9.25 bn in 2025, up 8% on 2024 and 22% on 2021 [USDA EG 2026].

Growth is selective. Coffee, chocolate and nut imports more than doubled or nearly so since 2021; beverages and pet food shrank as local plants took over [USDA EG 2026].

The bar rises now. Halal certification is mandatory for imported food from 18 Oct 2026 [PP 42/2024], and the rupiah is ~8% weaker than its 2025 average [JISDOR Sep 2026].

Enter with halal-certified premium snacks, sauces and foodservice ingredients through a strong licensed importer; avoid mass packaged lines.

What changed

The core buyer is shrinking. The middle class lost ~9.5 m people in five years to 2024 [USDA EG 2025]; premium baskets now rely on a smaller, richer group.

Foodservice is the growth buyer. Coffee and tea chains, bakery cafés and Japanese and Korean restaurants drive cheese, cream and beef demand [USDA EG 2026].

Muslims are the large majority of shoppers, so halal status decides reach beyond the expatriate and ethnic-Chinese core.

Sell to businesses as well as shoppers: foodservice buys year-round in volume, households buy in festive peaks.

Reading the mix

Two categories carry the bill. Dairy and fresh fruit are US$3.86 bn, 42% of the US$9.25 bn total; with beef, 53% [USDA EG 2026].

Dairy is an input more than a shelf item. About 80% of dairy is imported, mostly as powders, whey and cheese for processors and cafés [USDA EG 2025].

The long tail is where brands compete: snacks, sauces, sweet biscuits and ice cream are among the top ten growth lines [USDA EG 2026].

A branded entrant competes in the tail, where each category is US$0.1-0.4 bn and importers look for differentiated lines.

Chapter 2 · Growth and fade
012 / 041

Coffee and chocolate imports more than doubled in four years, while drinks, pet food and fresh vegetables shrank as local supply took over

Where is imported food still growing, and where do local products take over? The split follows local supply, not demand.

Change in import value by category, 2021 to 2025
% change, US$ basis · highlighted: the four fastest risers and the three categories in decline
Source: [USDA EG 2026], KIRA calculation

The answer to the hook

Still growing: inputs Indonesia lacks. Café coffee, cocoa, nuts and fries (no processing-grade potatoes) rose 60-163% [USDA EG 2026].

Taken over locally: drinks, pet food and fresh vegetables fell as local plants and farms scaled; dairy grew only 5% as free school meals push local milk [USDA EG 2025].

The rule we infer: imports grow where supply is structurally short and fade once a category passes the scale that pays for a local plant [Kira estimates].

Target categories where local supply is structurally short; avoid ones where a local plant already sets the price.

The US share of consumer food imports halved to 4.7% in four years as suppliers with free-trade access, led by China, took the growth

Tariff access and distance decide supplier share in commodity-like categories; brand matters only in the premium tail.

Who gains, who loses

Free-trade suppliers gain. Australia and New Zealand lead dairy and beef on lower tariffs and shorter transit; China leads fruit and now fries under RCEP [USDA EG 2026].

The US loses: US$705 m to US$439 m in four years while the market grew [USDA EG 2026].

Supply is concentrated by category, not overall: one origin holds ~3/4 of fruit and Australia dominates beef [USDA EG 2026].

Use RCEP and the bilateral agreements on every SKU: a tariff gap of even 5% decides importer choice in a price-led category.

Korea ships more than Japan; both are niche suppliers whose halal gap is now the binding constraint.

Small, but rising

Japan: ¥17.3 bn in 2025, rank 17 among destinations; top lines are sardines, scallops and koi carp [MAFF 2026]. Processed food (¥4.7 bn, ~US$30 m) is under 1% of Indonesia's ~US$6 bn processed food imports [Kira estimates].

Korea: US$246 m of farm and food exports in 2023 [Korea Times Dec 2024], +3.3% to Sep 2024 [MAFRA 2024]; only 252 of 1,412 major Korean products held halal certificates in late 2024 [Korea Times Dec 2024].

Brands, not trade, carry the presence: Indonesia is ~25% of Samyang's South-East Asian sales [JoongAng 2024].

The room for Japanese and Korean processed food is large relative to today's base, but only for halal-certified lines.

Chapter 3 · Price build-up
021 / 041

An imported snack roughly doubles in price between port and shelf; importer and retailer margins, not duties, take most of the gap

How to read it

Shelf is ~2.0-2.3x CIF. Duty is low for most RCEP and bilateral-agreement lines; margins, not tariffs, drive the multiple [Kira estimates].

Compliance is a fixed cost. Registration, halal and labels weigh most on small SKUs; certification takes 3-5 months [Dailian Jun 2026].

Currency adds to it: the rupiah traded at Rp17,500-17,900 per US$ in Sep 2026 against a 2025 average of Rp16,430 [JISDOR Sep 2026] [BI 2025].

Net effect

Foodservice is the strongest pull. A US$30 bn sector still adding coffee, tea and bakery chains [USDA EG 2026].

School meals help dairy, but locally first: the agency prioritises regions with dairy farms and plans cattle imports [USDA EG 2025]; spending reached Rp153 tn by Sep 2026 [Katadata Oct 2026].

Brakes bite early: currency and halal costs weigh on 2026-27; localisation weighs on mass lines throughout [Kira estimates].

Expect 2026-27 to be the slow years; enter now to be certified and listed before demand reaccelerates.

Play now: foodservice ingredients, premium snacks, sauces and café inputs; certifiable, specification-led, and where JP/KR origin is valued.
