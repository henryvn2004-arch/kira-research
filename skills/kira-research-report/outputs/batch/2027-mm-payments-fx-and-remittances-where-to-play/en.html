How we built this view

This view is built from two evidence streams: analyst research — English- and Burmese-language desk research, central bank notices, law-firm readings of new rules, company disclosures and analyst synthesis — and secondary anchors — multilateral statistics, listed company filings and triangulated estimates. Every quantitative claim carries an explicit source tag.

Analyst research

→ Local-language desk research
Burmese-language press and notices, where licence actions, exchange rates and crackdowns are often reported first.
→ Analyst synthesis
KIRA in-house triangulation across disclosed inputs. Tagged inline as [Kira estimates].
→ Rule readings
Central bank notifications read through two or more independent law-firm summaries; dates checked against each other.

Secondary anchors

→ Official and multilateral data
Central bank, World Bank, ADB and FATF releases, cited inline by named source.
→ Listed company filings
The only audited window on a Myanmar wallet: Wave Money results inside Yoma Strategic filings in Singapore.
→ Triangulated estimates
Where no single source covers a figure (wallet value, informal remittances), we combine 2-3 inputs and show the method. Tagged [Kira estimates].

Why the answer is "play from the edge"

The flow is large and digital. One wallet, Wave Money, moved MMK 48.9 trillion in the year to March 2026, over 10% of GDP [Yoma FY2026]; we put all wallet transfers at MMK 160-190 trillion [Kira estimates].

Rules, not demand, set the channel. Forced remittance rules lifted official inflows to US$5.6 bn in 2025 from US$0.67 bn in 2022 [Bloomberg May 2026], while a near-2x rate gap keeps hundi alive.

The onshore door is narrow. State FX banks are US-sanctioned [OFAC 2023] and FATF threatens countermeasures if progress stalls by Oct 2026 [FATF Jun 2026].

Start with a licensed send corridor from Japan or Korea paying into a vetted Myanmar wallet; keep onshore equity behind a FATF and sanctions gate.

The official rate has not moved since 2022 while the street rate doubled it, so most money chooses its price

Exporters, migrants and importers each face a different conversion rule, which makes "the" exchange rate a policy choice.

Official vs market kyat rate over time
MMK per USD · grey dot = official reference rate, blue dot = market or bank rate reported at that date
Source: [DVB 2022]; [AF 2023]; [Lincoln Sep 2024]; [Mekong Migration 2026]; [CBM Jun 2026]

The gap is the business model of hundi

The reference rate has stayed at 2,100 since August 2022 [CBM Jun 2026]; market readings have run 33-100% above it.

Banks now pay remitted wages near the market, about 4,150 in 2024 [Lincoln Sep 2024], but in 2023 the official baht rate was 56 kyat against about 100 on the street [Bangkok Post 2023]. That gap is what hundi brokers sell.

Implication: a licensed product wins only where it pays out close to the street rate.
Price every product off the market rate band (MMK 3,600-4,200) and treat the official rate as a tax on whoever is forced to use it.

A slow retreat from forced conversion

Notification 2/2026, announced 7 January and effective 1 January 2026, cut the share to 15% from 25% [A&G Jan 2026]. DFDL reads the remaining 85% as convertible at the online trading rate [DFDL Jan 2026].

Each cut narrows the implicit tax on exporters and the incentive to under-invoice; procedures under Notification 12/2022 still apply.

Caveat: the current CBM rate applied to the 15% was not confirmed in public sources; law-firm summaries, not the official text, were used.

Interest income rose 79% to US$7.9 m as users kept more money in their wallets, and core EBITDA still improved 15.6%. In the June 2026 quarter revenue fell another 20.8% while interest on trust balances added US$2.1 m [Yoma 1Q FY27].

Implication: the payout partner earns on float, so a sender that keeps money in the wallet is worth more to it than the transfer fee.
Offer payout partners float, not just volume: a corridor that lands money in wallet balances is the better bargaining chip.
