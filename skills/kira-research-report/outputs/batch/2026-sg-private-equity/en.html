How we built this view

This view is built from two evidence streams — primary inputs from transaction trackers, regulator disclosures and analyst synthesis, and secondary anchors from statutory data, exchange filings and tax-policy instruments. Singapore's business and regulatory press leads in English, so research ran on an English-language baseline. Every quantitative claim carries an explicit source tag.

Primary inputs

Transaction trackers
Quarterly and annual private equity deal-and-exit counts for Southeast Asia, read at both full-year and single-quarter granularity so that the 2025 trough and the 1Q 2026 rebound are visible separately. Cited inline by named source.
Manager & platform disclosures
Fund closes, platform commitments and named transactions from sponsors and sovereign investors active in the region. Cited by manager plus period.
Analyst synthesis
KIRA in-house triangulation across thirteen English-language research queries and pattern-matching against our regional financial-services work. Where no single source covers a figure, tagged [Kira estimates].

Secondary anchors

Regulator & statutory data
Monetary Authority of Singapore asset-management statistics, the Equity Market Development Programme announcements, and the Section 13O / 13U fund tax-incentive conditions as revised for 2025.
Exchange & capital-markets data
Singapore Exchange listing counts and pipeline commentary, plus secondary-market volume series covering GP-led transactions and continuation vehicles globally and in Asia.
Triangulated estimates
Singapore-booked deal value, prior-year comparatives derived from disclosed growth rates, and all forward scenarios. Tagged [Kira estimates] with the anchor metric disclosed inline.

Deployment recovered; liquidity did not

Southeast Asian sponsors deployed USD 9.1 bn across 59 deals in 2025, a 43% fall in value on a 12% fall in count — fewer megadeals, not fewer transactions, with four deals above USD 1 bn against eight a year earlier [EY SEA PE 2026]. The first quarter of 2026 then delivered USD 9.2 bn across 19 investments, the strongest quarter in five years [EY SEA PE 1Q26]. Realisations moved the other way: USD 4.4 bn across 33 exits in 2025, down 47% [EY SEA PE 2026].

Singapore books what the region originates

Singapore took 94% of first-quarter 2026 deal value and 68% of deal volume [EY SEA PE 1Q26], against more than 74% of full-year 2025 value [EY SEA PE 2026]. The stock behind the flow is larger still: Singapore-managed private equity and venture capital AUM reached SGD 789 bn at end-2024, up 20% year on year [MAS AM Survey 2025]. Regional risk is structured, held and increasingly warehoused here.

Five strategic implications for market participants

The region's problem is not sourcing and it is not capital. It is the ratio between what goes in and what can come out. Each implication below attaches to a number carried in the body of this report.

01
Underwrite the exit before the entry
Deployment ran at roughly 2.1x exit proceeds in 2025 [Kira estimates]. Committee papers that assume a listing or a strategic sale in year five are underwriting a channel that cleared USD 4.4 bn regionally [EY SEA PE 2026]. Name the buyer set at entry, or price the illiquidity.
Anchor: USD 9.1 bn in / USD 4.4 bn out, 2025
02
Build continuation-vehicle capability now
GP-led secondaries reached USD 106 bn globally in 2025, from USD 71 bn [PitchBook 2026], while Asia holds only about 3% of secondary volume [PwC SG 2026]. The capability gap is the opportunity: sponsors with a credible single-asset process convert an ageing book into DPI on their own timetable.
Anchor: Asia = ~3% of global secondary volume
03
Treat the listing window as mid-cap, not mega-cap
Singapore listed 15 new equities in a half-year against five a year earlier [SGX 1H 2026], and state-backed placement capital is mandated toward small and mid caps [MAS EQDP 2026]. That reflates the exit route for SGD 200 m-800 m assets, not for the USD 1 bn-plus infrastructure platforms now dominating deployment.
Anchor: SGD 6.5 bn EQDP, small/mid-cap mandate
04
Re-test domicile against substance, not habit
Section 13W share-disposal relief is now permanent [SG Budget 2025], but the 15% domestic top-up tax applies to groups above EUR 750 m of revenue from 2025 [MNE Min Tax Act 2024]. The Singapore holding structure still wins on certainty — it no longer wins on headline rate alone.
Anchor: 15% minimum effective rate, from 1 Jan 2025
05
Price the concentration you are inheriting
Infrastructure took 77% of first-quarter 2026 deal value, with consumer at 14% and technology at 7% [EY SEA PE 1Q26]. A regional allocation made today is largely a bet on data-centre and energy build-out. Diversify deliberately or state the beta to the investment committee.
Anchor: Infrastructure 77% of 1Q26 value

Section 05 · Capital pools
007 / 019

From the Asia-Pacific pool to Singapore's booked share

Southeast Asia is a small slice of Asia-Pacific private equity — roughly 17% of regional deal value in the first quarter of 2026 — but an unusually concentrated one. Nearly all of the region's value passes through a single jurisdiction, which is what makes Singapore's channel capacity a regional problem rather than a local one.

Asia-Pacific: dry powder off its peak

Regional dry powder fell to roughly USD 240 bn, down from USD 278 bn in 2024 [Deloitte APAC 2026]. That is a healthy signal, not a weak one: capital is converting to deals faster than it is being replaced. Net cash flow to investors turned positive for the first time since 2021 [Deloitte APAC 2026].

Southeast Asia: the 17% slice

Southeast Asia accounted for about 17% of Asia-Pacific deal value in the first quarter of 2026, on USD 9.2 bn across 19 transactions [EY SEA PE 1Q26]. Regional dry powder sits at USD 21.1 bn against 2025 fundraising of USD 3.3 bn [DealStreetAsia 2026] — roughly six years of cover at last year's deployment pace [Kira estimates].

Singapore: where the value books

Singapore took 94% of first-quarter value and 68% of transaction count [EY SEA PE 1Q26]. The gap between those two numbers is the whole story: the large tickets are Singapore-domiciled platforms, while smaller deals stay spread across Indonesia, Vietnam, Malaysia and the Philippines.

Section 06 · Deployment pattern
008 / 019

One theme is absorbing the capital

Infrastructure — overwhelmingly digital infrastructure — took 42% of 2025 deployment and 77% of the first quarter of 2026 [EY SEA PE 2026; EY SEA PE 1Q26]. Consumer and technology are no longer the region's headline story. Power, land and fibre are.

The mix has narrowed, not broadened

In 2025 the split was infrastructure 42%, telecommunications 12%, real estate 10% and energy 10% [EY SEA PE 2026] — already three-quarters of value in asset-heavy categories. By the first quarter of 2026, infrastructure alone held 77% of value, with consumer at 14% and technology at 7% [EY SEA PE 1Q26]. Ticket size, not deal count, drives the shift: 19 deals produced USD 9.2 bn [EY SEA PE 1Q26].

Sovereign and state-linked capital sets the pace

The largest 2025 transactions were platform-scale and sovereign-anchored: GLP at USD 1.5 bn, Princeton Digital Group at USD 1.3 bn, Yinson Production Offshore at USD 1.0 bn and True Internet Data Center at USD 1.0 bn [EY SEA PE 2026]. Vantage Data Centers closed USD 1.6 bn of equity into its Asia-Pacific platform, led by Singapore and Abu Dhabi sovereign investors [Vantage 2025].

The cross-border power arbitrage

Johor's industrial tariff of about 13.5 US cents/kWh against Singapore's 23.9 [Knight Frank 2025] is why the compute sits over the causeway while the capital, the holdco and the fee line sit in Singapore. Sponsors underwrite a Malaysian asset through a Singapore vehicle — and will exit it the same way.

Digital infrastructure platforms

Section 09 · Exit channel capacity
011 / 019

Two dollars in for every dollar out

Regional realisations fell to USD 4.4 bn across 33 exits in 2025, down 47% on value while exit count rose 18% [EY SEA PE 2026]. Smaller exits, more often. Against USD 9.1 bn deployed, the region ran a deployment-to-realisation ratio of roughly 2.1x [Kira estimates].

The arithmetic of the overhang

Exit value has now fallen for two consecutive years while deal count has held. Across Asia-Pacific, the number of portfolio companies held beyond five years rose 18% against 2024 [Deloitte APAC 2026], concentrated in the 2020-22 vintages bought at peak multiples. Singapore is where most of that regional book is domiciled, so the ageing sits on Singapore-managed balance sheets.

Route mix: trade sale carries the load

Trade sales remain the dominant route [EY SEA PE 2026]. Sponsor-to-sponsor and secondary transactions produced 35 exits in 2025, the highest count since 2020, and PE-backed listings produced nine, raising about USD 1.39 bn [DealStreetAsia 2026]. Counting conventions differ across trackers; the direction — more routes, smaller tickets — does not.

The first quarter did not fix it

Six exits generated USD 1.7 bn in the first quarter of 2026 [EY SEA PE 1Q26]. Annualised, that is a materially better year than 2025 — and still roughly a third of the quarter's USD 9.2 bn of deployment [Kira estimates]. The gap is the constraint this report is named for.

Section 10 · The listing channel
012 / 019

The listing window reopened — at mid-cap width

Singapore's exchange logged 15 new equity listings in a half-year against five a year earlier [SGX 1H 2026], on the back of a state-backed programme that has now grown to SGD 6.5 bn [MAS EQDP 2026]. The reflation is real. It is also mandated toward the small and mid caps that most regional sponsors are not currently buying.

What reopened the window

The Equity Market Development Programme launched in February 2025 with SGD 5 bn of placement capacity and was expanded to SGD 6.5 bn in February 2026 [MAS EQDP 2026]. SGD 3.95 bn has been placed across nine appointed managers, with a further tranche expected in the second quarter of 2026 [MAS EQDP 2026]. Participating strategies must invest predominantly in Singapore-listed equities with a small- and mid-cap focus.

The effect on primary issuance was immediate. Singapore led ASEAN on IPO proceeds in 2025, raising about USD 2.15 bn — the most since 2017 [Malay Mail 2026], led by two large REIT listings. A single-prospectus fast track for concurrent Singapore and Nasdaq listings is scheduled to go live by mid-2026 [Malay Mail 2026].

MAS has also committed SGD 50 m to equity research and product-listing support, addressing the coverage thinness that has historically penalised smaller issuers [MAS EQDP 2025].

What it does not solve

Nine PE-backed listings across Southeast Asia raised roughly USD 1.39 bn in 2025 [DealStreetAsia 2026] — the best year since 2020, and still less than a third of the region's total exit proceeds. The channel is open at a width that clears mid-market consumer, healthcare and industrial assets. It does not clear a USD 1 bn-plus data-centre platform.

  • Scale mismatch — the lane taking 77% of deployment [EY SEA PE 1Q26] has no realistic domestic listing route.
  • Crowding-out risk — a reflated secondary market raises the return hurdle a new issue must clear to attract the same buyer.
  • REIT dependence — 2025's headline proceeds came from stabilised-asset REIT structures, not operating-company floats [Malay Mail 2026].
  • Programme duration — placement capital is finite; SGD 1.05 bn of the original SGD 5 bn remained unplaced entering 2026 [MAS EQDP 2026].

Our read: treat the window as a two-to-three-year opportunity for SGD 200 m-800 m enterprise values [Kira estimates], and do not build a platform-scale exit case on it.

Section 11 · Secondaries
013 / 019

The release valve Asia has barely opened

Global secondary volume hit a record USD 226 bn in 2025, with GP-led transactions at USD 106 bn [PitchBook 2026]. Asia accounts for roughly 3% of that [PwC SG 2026]. For a region holding an ageing book in a jurisdiction built for fund structuring, the under-use is the single clearest capability gap.

The global market set the template

GP-led volume rose from USD 71 bn to USD 106 bn between 2024 and 2025, and the first half of 2026 alone cleared more than USD 120 bn of total secondary volume, up 20% year on year [PitchBook 2026]. Single-asset continuation vehicles accounted for USD 34 bn of first-half value — over half of GP-led activity [PitchBook 2026].

Asia's usage is different in kind

Where North American sponsors use continuation vehicles to hold trophy assets, Asia-Pacific managers are far more likely to use them for problem-asset rollovers — 24% cite that use case against 5% in North America [Sec Mkt Survey 2026]. That reputational framing is itself a brake on adoption, and it is one that pricing discipline can unwind.

Singapore is the natural venue

The fund-structuring stack, the LP relationships and the advisory bench are already here [PwC SG 2026]. Projections put continuation vehicles at 30-40% of all private equity exits globally over 2026-27 [Preqin 2026]. Applying even the low end of that to the Southeast Asian book implies a step-change in regional CV formation [Kira estimates].

Section 13 · Fund structure & tax
015 / 019

The structuring stack after the minimum tax

Three instruments hold the Singapore case together: the Variable Capital Company as fund vehicle, the Section 13O and 13U incentives as the tax wrapper, and permanent Section 13W relief on share disposals. A 15% minimum effective rate from 2025 changes the arithmetic for large groups without displacing the structure.

The vehicle and the wrapper

The Variable Capital Company, in force since January 2020, was purpose-built for funds and now numbers well over a thousand incorporations [VCC Act 2020]. Sub-funds ring-fence assets and liabilities, which is what makes it workable for multi-strategy platforms and for continuation structures carved out of an existing book.

On the tax wrapper, Section 13U is the usual home for a private equity fund: a minimum SGD 50 m of AUM, at least three investment professionals, and tiered local business spending. Section 13O serves smaller or first-time funds below that threshold [MAS 13O/13U 2025]. Both sets of conditions were raised with effect from 1 January 2025 — a deliberate quality filter, not a revenue measure.

Family offices sit alongside this stack rather than inside it. Single family offices in Singapore passed 1,400 in 2025, from fewer than 400 in 2020 [MAS FO Data 2025], and now form a material co-investment base for regional sponsors.

What the minimum tax changes

Singapore applied a domestic top-up tax and a multinational top-up tax from 1 January 2025, lifting the effective rate to 15% for groups with revenue at or above EUR 750 m in two of the preceding four years [MNE Min Tax Act 2024]. Fund vehicles themselves are generally outside scope; large portfolio groups often are not.

  • Section 13W made permanent — gains on qualifying share disposals stay exempt, with the 2027 sunset removed [SG Budget 2025].
  • No capital gains tax — the foundational reason regional holdcos sit here, unchanged by Pillar Two.
  • No withholding on outbound dividends — the reason Singapore remains the preferred regional holding jurisdiction [Deloitte APAC 2026].
  • Substance is now the test — investment-professional headcount and local spend conditions are checked, not assumed [MAS 13O/13U 2025].

The practical consequence for 2026 vintages: model the 15% floor at the portfolio-group level and keep the fund-level analysis separate. Structures that relied on a sub-15% blended rate in a third jurisdiction are the ones that need re-papering, not the Singapore holdco itself [Kira estimates].

Faster diligence, unchanged accountability

Deal teams have moved past pilots. 88% of private equity teams have committed at least USD 1 m to generative AI, above the 83% average across M&A teams [Deloitte M&A AI 2026]. The gains are concentrated in preparation work — sourcing, document review, portfolio monitoring — not in the investment decision itself.

Where the spend is landing

Among adopters, 40% apply it to strategy and market assessment, 35% to target identification and 35% to due diligence [Deloitte M&A AI 2026]. Nearly half of dealmakers now use these tools close to daily [Sourcescrub 2026].

Why Southeast Asia benefits disproportionately

Regional diligence is document-heavy and multi-jurisdictional: filings in four languages, informal accounts, thin comparable sets. Machine-assisted extraction compresses the slowest part of a Southeast Asian process — the first four weeks [Kira estimates].

What does not transfer

Underwriting judgement, related-party diagnosis and the exit-buyer call remain human. In a market where the exit is the constraint, faster screening without better exit judgement simply builds the ageing book quicker.

Deployment and realisations to 2031

The deployment line is the easier call: one strong quarter plus USD 21.1 bn of regional dry powder [DealStreetAsia 2026] sets a floor. The realisation line is the variable. Our scenarios turn on three swing factors — listing-window durability, continuation-vehicle adoption, and whether infrastructure ticket sizes stay this large.

Base case
USD 22-26bn 2026
Regional deployment, with Singapore booking 80-90% [Kira estimates]. Exits recover to USD 7-9 bn as the listing window holds and sponsor-to-sponsor volume builds. The deployment-to-realisation ratio holds near 2.9x, wider than 2025's 2.1x [Kira estimates].
Downside
USD 14-17bn 2026
Infrastructure megadeals pause on power or tariff friction, and the 1Q 2026 print proves to be a one-quarter concentration effect. Exits stall near USD 5 bn and the ageing book compounds, forcing price discovery through discounted secondaries [Kira estimates].
Upside
USD 30-34bn 2026
Sovereign co-investment sustains platform-scale tickets, the Nasdaq dual-listing fast track adds a genuine second listing venue [Malay Mail 2026], and regional continuation-vehicle formation moves toward the global 30-40% share of exits [Preqin 2026].

What moves the number

  • Ticket concentration — 19 deals produced USD 9.2 bn in 1Q 2026 [EY SEA PE 1Q26]; a handful of deferrals moves the annual line by a third.
  • Listing-window width — SGD 6.5 bn of placement capacity is finite and small-cap mandated [MAS EQDP 2026].
  • CV adoption — Asia at ~3% of global secondary volume [PwC SG 2026] is the largest single lever on realisations.

The directional read

Southeast Asian private equity has a healthy front end and a constrained back end, and Singapore sits at both. Our base case assumes the region deploys well and realises slowly, with the gap closed by structural innovation rather than by the public markets. Sponsors that build continuation and sponsor-to-sponsor capability during 2026-27 will set the regional DPI benchmark for the vintage — the through-line of this report.

Methodology & sources

How this view was built, how to read the source tags, and the full citation list for every named source used across the report.

Methodology

This view triangulates two evidence streams. Where a figure carries a named source tag (e.g. [EY SEA PE 1Q26], [MAS AM Survey 2025]), the underlying data comes from that source; the full citation appears at right. Where a figure carries [Kira estimates], our analysts derived it from a blend of disclosed inputs — transaction trackers, regulator statistics, exchange data and regional pattern-matching — with the anchor metric disclosed inline.

Two conventions matter for reading the numbers. First, prior-year comparatives (2024 deployment and exit value) are derived from disclosed year-on-year change rates, not separately published, and are tagged accordingly. Second, exit counts differ across trackers because "secondary exit" and "PE-backed exit" are defined differently; we cite each source's own count rather than reconciling them into a single series. Approximately 25-30% of quantitative claims are KIRA-derived; the remainder are externally cited.

Research ran on an English-language baseline across thirteen queries. Singapore's business and regulatory press leads in English, so no local-language query pass was required.
